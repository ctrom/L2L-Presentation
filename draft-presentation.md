# Revitalizing TechNovation Solutions
## AI Clinical Decision Support: Funding Proposal

*Presentation Draft Script — 15-Minute Executive Pitch*

---

## Team & Presentation Order

| Speaker | Project Role | Presentation Section |
|---|---|---|
| **Christopher** | Technical/AI Lead | Opening Hook + AI Technology Description & Value Proposition (technical angle) |
| **Margie** | Clinical Liaison + Project Manager | Value Proposition (clinical angle) + Project Plan & Milestones |
| **Ashley** | Finance Lead | Funding Request & ROI Model |
| **Dane** | Sales/BD Lead + Compliance/Risk Lead | Team Composition (wrap-up) + Potential Challenges & Executive Champion + Closing Ask |

Total time budget: 15 minutes, with buffer built into each section for leadership Q&A interruptions.

**The Ask:** $3.18M in partial funding, an Executive Champion, and 18–24 months to break-even.

---

## 1. Christopher — Opening Hook + AI Technology Description & Value Proposition

*Target time: ~4–4.5 minutes*

**Opening Hook (~45 seconds)**

"Five years ago, TechNovation was the name hospital systems trusted first. Today, we're losing ground — not because our core products stopped working, but because we stopped moving as fast as the market did. Our customers have told us clearly: they need more than what we're currently offering. Today, we're proposing a $3.18 million investment in an AI-powered clinical decision support tool — one that we can put in front of our existing clients within six months, and use to win new ones within twelve. This isn't a moonshot. It's the fastest, lowest-risk path back to market leadership. Let me show you how."

**Technology Description (~2 minutes)**

"Here's what we're proposing, in simple terms: an AI clinical decision support layer that integrates directly into the product our clients already use every day.

We're not building this model from scratch. We're licensing and customizing the technology behind OpenEvidence, a clinical decision support platform already running in production at major health systems — including Mount Sinai and Cedars-Sinai, where it's integrated directly into their Epic EHR environments. That matters: this isn't an unproven idea we're asking you to bet on. It's a platform with a track record, and we're bringing that same capability into TechNovation's own product.

When a clinician enters a patient's symptoms and history, the system cross-references that information against millions of similar clinical cases and current medical literature — in real time — and surfaces the most likely diagnoses, flags potential drug interactions, and recommends next steps. It doesn't replace the clinician's judgment. It gives them a second set of eyes, instantly, at the exact moment they're making a decision.

Because we're building this as an extension of our existing platform — not a separate product — our current clients don't need to rip out and replace anything. They upgrade. That's a critical point: it dramatically lowers the barrier to adoption compared to a brand-new tool from a competitor."

**Value Proposition (~1.5 minutes)**

"This directly answers the two problems named in our own market feedback: customers say we've been slow to innovate, and they've felt that gap. This closes it — visibly, and fast.

For our clients, the value is concrete: fewer diagnostic errors, faster clinical decisions, better patient outcomes — all things a hospital system can show its own board. For us, it means something just as important: we're no longer just a legacy vendor they tolerate. We're the partner helping them modernize.

And competitively, this matters right now. The vendors currently ahead of us in this space are largely still offering static, rules-based systems — not adaptive AI. We have a window to leapfrog them, but that window won't stay open long."

**Transition (~15 seconds)**

"So that's what the technology does, and why it matters. Margie is going to walk you through how this plays out clinically, and how we get from here to a fully validated product."

**Delivery notes:**
- The opening reuses the scenario's own language on declining market share and dissatisfaction — signals the team has internalized the panel's problem, not brought a generic pitch.
- Naming OpenEvidence and its existing health-system deployments is a deliberate credibility move — it turns "trust us, this technology works" into "here's where it's already working."
- Technical explanation avoids ML jargon (no "training data," "neural networks") to stay executive-level.
- Keep the transition line format consistent across all four speakers — reinforces rehearsed teamwork (ties to the "Team preparation" rubric criterion).

## 2. Margie — Clinical Value Proposition + Project Plan & Milestones

*Target time: ~4 minutes*

**Clinical Value Proposition (~1.5 minutes)**

"Thank you, Christopher. I want to build on that by grounding this in what actually happens on the ground, in a clinic or hospital floor.

Right now, our existing clients are telling us the same thing in different words: their clinicians are stretched thin, decision-making happens under time pressure, and errors — even small ones — carry real cost, both financially and in patient trust. This tool doesn't ask clinicians to change how they work. It sits inside the workflow they already have, and gives them a faster, more confident path to the same decisions they're already making.

That matters for adoption. We're not asking hospital systems to retrain their staff on a new system. We're asking them to let the system they already trust get smarter. That's a much easier conversation to have with a skeptical clinical staff — and it's exactly the kind of low-friction win that turns an existing client into a reference customer for new business."

**Project Plan & Milestones (~2.5 minutes)**

"So here's how we get there, and we've built this timeline deliberately to prove value early rather than ask for trust upfront.

Months one through six are our build phase. Christopher's team customizes and integrates the OpenEvidence-licensed model into our existing platform, we stand up the data infrastructure on our existing Microsoft Azure environment, and — critically — we begin compliance groundwork in parallel with Clearwater, our HIPAA compliance partner, not as an afterthought. By month six, we have a working demo ready to put in front of our existing clients.

Months six through twelve are our pilot phase. We launch with three to five of our current hospital system clients — chosen specifically because they already trust us and already run our platform, which means less integration risk and faster time to signal. During this window, we're running clinical validation side by side with real clinicians, refining the tool based on their feedback, and finalizing compliance sign-off with Clearwater. By month twelve, we don't just have a finished product — we have real outcomes data and case studies from clients willing to vouch for it.

From month twelve on, we shift into expansion: rolling this out more broadly across our existing client base, and — using those pilot results as proof — beginning to pitch new clients who haven't worked with us before.

We've built this sequence intentionally. Every phase creates the evidence the next phase needs. By the time we're asking a brand-new client to trust this technology, we're not asking them to take our word for it — we're showing them results from hospital systems just like theirs."

**Transition (~15 seconds)**

"Now that you've seen how this comes to life, Ashley is going to walk you through what it costs, and what it returns."

**Delivery notes:**
- "Asking the system they already trust to get smarter" does double duty — reinforces low-friction adoption while implicitly countering the clinician-resistance risk before Dane raises it later.
- Naming Clearwater and Azure here reinforces that compliance and infrastructure aren't afterthoughts — they're staffed by named specialists from month one.
- Milestone phase language matches Christopher's transition and the timeline exactly, so the story doesn't feel restructured mid-pitch.

## 3. Ashley — Funding Request & ROI Model

*Target time: ~4 minutes*

**Framing the Ask (~30 seconds)**

"Thank you, Margie. I'm going to walk you through the numbers behind this proposal — what we're asking for, where it goes, and when it pays off.

We're requesting **$3.18 million** to fund this project through completion and pilot. Every dollar in this number traces to a named vendor or a specific role and hour count — not a round estimate. We're asking for exactly enough to build this right and prove it works with real clients and real results."

**Cost Breakdown (~1.5 minutes)**

"Here's where that funding goes:"

- $1.16 million — AI model licensing and customization with OpenEvidence, the clinical decision support platform already running at health systems like Mount Sinai and Cedars-Sinai. This covers the licensing fee plus their implementation specialists' time adapting it to our platform.
- $681,000 — Integration engineering, connecting OpenEvidence into our existing platform, combining our own engineers with contract support for surge capacity.
- $498,000 — Data infrastructure and compliance: our existing Microsoft Azure for Healthcare environment, plus Clearwater, a healthcare-only HIPAA compliance firm, handling our security risk analysis and audit readiness.
- $607,000 — Our core leadership team over the twelve-month build and pilot period.
- $236,000 — Pilot support contingency, so we plan for the unexpected rather than ask for more later.

"That totals $3.18 million — a request scaled to what this project actually needs, and one where the panel can trace every dollar back to a named partner or a specific role."

**Revenue Model (~1.5 minutes)**

"Now, how this pays for itself. Our pricing model is straightforward: $150 per provider, per month — simple enough that any hospital administrator can do the math on what this costs them, and simple enough for us to project with confidence.

Our existing client base gives us roughly 3,000 provider seats across our current hospital systems. We're not assuming full adoption on day one — we're modeling a realistic ramp:"

- By month 12 (pilot clients live): ~450 providers onboarded — roughly $67,500/month in recurring revenue
- By month 18 (existing base scaling + first new clients): ~1,400 providers — $210,000/month
- By month 24 (broader adoption + growing new-client pipeline): ~2,200 providers — $330,000/month

"Because every cost in this budget is grounded in real vendor pricing and realistic staffing hours, this revenue ramp gets us to break-even comfortably inside our eighteen-to-twenty-four-month target, with healthy margin for error."

**Closing the Section (~30 seconds)**

"I want to be direct about something: these projections assume steady execution, not a best-case scenario. We built this model to be defensible, not impressive — because the worst outcome here isn't a modest return, it's overpromising and underdelivering in front of the very clients we're trying to win back."

**Transition (~15 seconds)**

"With the financial case laid out, Dane is going to close by covering our team, the risks we're watching for, and what we're asking of this leadership team specifically."

**Delivery notes:**
- "Defensible, not impressive" is a deliberate hedge — pre-empts a "these numbers seem too good" pushback before it's asked.
- Leading with "every dollar traces to a named vendor or role" pre-empts the most likely panel pushback on a funding ask: "how did you get these numbers?"
- If asked for the exact break-even month, Ashley should be ready to say "inside the 20–22 month range, based on this revenue ramp and budget" rather than an artificially precise single number.

## 4. Dane — Team, Challenges, Executive Champion & Closing Ask

*Target time: ~3–3.5 minutes*

**Team Composition Wrap-Up (~30 seconds)**

"Thank you, Ashley. Before I close, I want to briefly speak to who's in this room with you today, and who we've chosen to work alongside, because execution matters as much as the plan itself.

Christopher leads the technical build. Margie bridges the clinical and operational side, keeping us grounded in real workflows and real timelines. Ashley owns the financial discipline you just heard. And I've been focused on client relationships — both keeping our existing partners engaged through this rollout, and building the pipeline for what comes next. Four people, four disciplines, one plan — supported by named specialist partners in OpenEvidence and Clearwater exactly where outside expertise beats an in-house learning curve. And we've deliberately built a hiring path into later phases so we're not overextending this core team before revenue catches up."

**Potential Challenges & Mitigations (~1.5 minutes)**

"I want to be upfront about what could go wrong here, because we'd rather name these risks ourselves than have them surface as questions.

Clinician adoption is our biggest risk. Physicians can be skeptical of AI in a clinical setting. That's exactly why we're piloting with clinicians directly, co-designing based on their feedback, and positioning this tool as decision support — never a replacement for clinical judgment. The physician always makes the final call.

Regulatory and compliance exposure is real in healthcare AI. That's why Clearwater is engaged from month one, not month eleven — compliance work runs in parallel with the technical build, not after it, with a firm whose entire practice is HIPAA risk analysis and OCR audit readiness.

Integration complexity is why we're starting with clients already on our existing platform, and why we're extending our existing Microsoft Azure environment rather than standing up a new infrastructure provider — we're not asking anyone to rebuild their infrastructure, and we're not building ours from zero either.

And liability — if an AI recommendation is ever connected to a bad outcome — is addressed the same way throughout: this tool advises, it doesn't decide. That's built into our contracts, our training, and how we message this externally, consistently."

**Executive Champion (~1 minute)**

"To manage these risks well, we're asking for more than funding — we're asking for a partner. We need an Executive Champion, ideally someone at the level of Chief Medical Officer or VP of Clinical Innovation. Someone who carries credibility with clinicians, so adoption resistance softens. Someone who can speak peer-to-peer with our clients' own clinical leadership, so pilot conversations move faster. And someone willing to stand behind this project internally, especially in month twelve or fifteen, if results are still ramping up and the room needs a reminder of why we started this."

**Closing Ask (~30 seconds)**

"So here's what we're asking today. Three point one eight million dollars, invested over twelve months, working with named partners already proven in this space — OpenEvidence for the technology, Clearwater for compliance — to build and pilot a tool that's proven with our own clients before we ever ask a new one to take a chance on us. A committed Executive Champion to help us move faster and manage risk along the way. And eighteen to twenty-four months to prove that TechNovation isn't just protecting its market position — it's setting the pace again.

We're not asking you to bet on a hypothesis. We're asking you to fund the fastest, lowest-risk path back to leadership, with partners already proven in this exact market. Thank you — we're glad to take your questions."

**Delivery notes:**
- "We'd rather name these risks ourselves than have them surface as questions" directly plays into the rubric's "Nimbleness" criterion.
- Re-naming OpenEvidence and Clearwater in the closing ask reinforces the "proven, not speculative" theme one last time right before Q&A opens.
- Closing ask restates the funding number, timeline, and Executive Champion ask in one breath — a clean, memorable summary right before Q&A opens.
